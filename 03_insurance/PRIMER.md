term used in these use cases, in plain English.

## How it works

**How an insurer makes money:** customers pay a *premium* to transfer a risk (a car crash, a burglary) to the
insurer. The insurer pools many customers' premiums and pays the *claims* of the few who suffer a loss. Profit comes
from pricing risk correctly (premiums > claims + costs) and from investing premiums before claims are paid.

**What makes it hard:**
1. **Pricing (underwriting):** charge each customer the right premium for their risk. Too low loses money; too high loses customers.
2. **Claims:** pay genuine claims quickly and fairly, while catching fraud, which adds costs for every honest customer.
3. **Clarity:** policies are long legal documents. Customers need clear answers about what they are covered for.
4. **Reserves and catastrophes:** claims are paid over years, so insurers must set aside enough money for claims not yet
   paid (or even reported), and make sure one big storm or flood can't sink the company, using *reinsurance*.

## Glossary

### Basics

- <a id="add-on-cover"></a>**Add-on cover**: Optional extra cover bought on top of the core policy, e.g. accidental damage.
- <a id="excess-deductible"></a>**Excess (deductible)**: The amount the customer pays towards each claim before the insurer pays the rest.
- <a id="exclusion"></a>**Exclusion**: Something the policy specifically does not cover, e.g. wear and tear.
- <a id="policy-wording--schedule"></a>**Policy wording / schedule**: The wording is the full contract (what is and isn't covered); the schedule lists this customer's chosen covers and limits.
- <a id="premium"></a>**Premium**: The price a customer pays for an insurance policy, usually yearly or monthly.
- <a id="sum-insured"></a>**Sum insured**: The maximum the insurer will pay for a policy or item.

### Claims

- <a id="adjuster"></a>**Adjuster**: The claims professional who assesses a claim and decides what the insurer pays.
- <a id="bodily-injury-claim"></a>**Bodily injury claim**: A claim for someone's injury, handled by a specialist team because it is costly and slow to settle.
- <a id="claim"></a>**Claim**: A customer's request for payment after a loss covered by the policy.
- <a id="claims-leakage"></a>**Claims leakage**: Money paid on claims that should not have been, through mistakes or missed recoveries rather than fraud.
- <a id="claims-triage"></a>**Claims triage**: Sorting new claims by complexity and risk: fast-track simple ones, send complex ones to experts.
- <a id="fnol"></a>**FNOL**: First Notice of Loss: the first report of a claim to the insurer.
- <a id="insurance-fraud"></a>**Insurance fraud**: Exaggerating or inventing a claim. Fraud costs insurers billions, raising premiums for everyone.
- <a id="market-value"></a>**Market value**: What it would cost to buy the same car (make, model, age, mileage) just before the loss.
- <a id="red-flag"></a>**Red flag**: A warning sign of possible fraud, e.g. a claim soon after the policy started or no proof of ownership.
- <a id="salvage"></a>**Salvage**: The damaged car's remaining value, e.g. for parts. The insurer sells it unless the customer keeps it.
- <a id="siu"></a>**SIU**: Special Investigations Unit: the team that investigates suspected fraud.
- <a id="subrogation"></a>**Subrogation**: Recovering what the insurer paid from a third party who caused the loss, e.g. the at-fault driver's insurer.
- <a id="total-loss-write-off"></a>**Total loss (write-off)**: When repairing the car would cost too much compared with its value, so the insurer pays out its value instead.
- <a id="vulnerable-customer"></a>**Vulnerable customer**: A customer whose circumstances (age, health, a crisis) mean they need extra care and support.
- <a id="wear-and-tear-betterment"></a>**Wear and tear (betterment)**: A deduction for an old item's age when the policy pays its value, so the customer isn't better off than before.
- <a id="write-off-category"></a>**Write-off category**: How badly a written-off car is damaged: Category S (structural) and N (non-structural) can be repaired; A and B must be scrapped.

### Pricing

- <a id="fair-pricing-rule"></a>**Fair-pricing rule**: A rule (e.g. in the UK since 2022) that renewing customers can't be charged more than a new customer for the same cover.
- <a id="price-walking"></a>**Price walking**: Raising prices for loyal customers each year because they rarely shop around. Banned by fair-pricing rules.
- <a id="rating-engine"></a>**Rating engine**: The system that calculates a premium from a base rate and rating factors.
- <a id="rating-factor"></a>**Rating factor**: A characteristic that changes the premium, e.g. driver age, car group or postcode.
- <a id="renewal"></a>**Renewal**: Offering an existing customer a new policy year, usually at a new price.
- <a id="technical-price"></a>**Technical price**: The price that covers the expected claims and costs for a risk. Below it, the policy is expected to lose money.
- <a id="underwriting"></a>**Underwriting**: Assessing a risk and deciding whether to insure it and on what terms.
- <a id="vehicle-insurance-group"></a>**Vehicle (insurance) group**: A 1-50 scale ranking cars by repair cost, performance and security. Higher groups cost more to insure.
- <a id="voluntary-excess"></a>**Voluntary excess**: An extra excess the customer chooses to pay on claims in exchange for a lower premium.

### Underwriting

- <a id="policy-terms-endorsement"></a>**Policy terms (endorsement)**: Special conditions added to one customer's policy, e.g. a higher flood excess.
- <a id="rebuild-cost"></a>**Rebuild cost**: What it would cost to rebuild the home from scratch. This, not the market price, sets the buildings sum insured.
- <a id="referral"></a>**Referral**: Passing a risk the rules can't approve on their own to a more senior underwriter.
- <a id="risk-appetite"></a>**Risk appetite**: The types and amount of risk the insurer is willing to accept, set by the board.
- <a id="subsidence"></a>**Subsidence**: Ground under a building sinking, which cracks walls. Expensive to fix, so insurers check its history.
- <a id="underinsurance"></a>**Underinsurance**: Insuring something for less than it is worth. The insurer may then pay only part of each claim.

### Actuarial

- <a id="1-in-200-event"></a>**1-in-200 event**: An event so severe it is expected once in 200 years (0.5% a year). Capital rules require surviving one.
- <a id="accident-year"></a>**Accident year**: The year in which the losses happened, however many years the claims take to pay.
- <a id="accumulation"></a>**Accumulation**: Too much insured value concentrated in one area, so a single event hits many policies at once.
- <a id="catastrophe-cat-event"></a>**Catastrophe (cat) event**: One event, such as a storm or flood, that causes many claims at once.
- <a id="chain-ladder"></a>**Chain-ladder**: The standard reserving method: assumes claims will keep building up the way they did in past years.
- <a id="claims-reserve"></a>**Claims reserve**: Money set aside today for claims that will be paid in the future.
- <a id="claims-triangle"></a>**Claims triangle**: A table of claims paid by accident year (rows) and years since (columns), used to see how claims build up over time.
- <a id="development-factor"></a>**Development factor**: How much claims paid for an accident year grow from one year to the next, e.g. 1.25 = +25%.
- <a id="earned-premium"></a>**Earned premium**: The part of premiums that covers time already passed, matched against that period's claims.
- <a id="excess-of-loss-xl"></a>**Excess of loss (XL)**: Reinsurance that pays the part of an event's loss above a retention, up to a limit, e.g. 100m excess of 25m.
- <a id="ibnr"></a>**IBNR**: Incurred But Not Reported: claims that have happened but the insurer hasn't heard about yet.
- <a id="loss-ratio"></a>**Loss ratio**: Claims divided by earned premium. Above about 75%, there is usually little left after costs.
- <a id="reinsurance"></a>**Reinsurance**: Insurance for insurers: another company pays part of very large losses in exchange for a premium.
- <a id="total-insured-value-tiv"></a>**Total insured value (TIV)**: The sum of all the sums insured in an area: the most the insurer could lose there.
- <a id="ultimate-claims"></a>**Ultimate claims**: The total an accident year's claims will cost once every claim is paid.

*Definitions are simplified for learning. Exact rules vary by country and regulator.*
