## How it works

**How an insurer makes money:** customers pay a *premium* to transfer a risk (a car crash, a burglary) to the
insurer. The insurer pools many customers' premiums and pays the *claims* of the few who suffer a loss. Profit comes
from pricing risk correctly (premiums > claims + costs) and from investing premiums before claims are paid.

**What makes it hard:**
1. **Pricing (underwriting):** charge each customer the right premium for their risk. Too low loses money; too high loses customers.
2. **Claims:** pay genuine claims quickly and fairly, while catching fraud, which adds costs for every honest customer.
3. **Clarity:** policies are long legal documents. Customers need clear answers about what they are covered for.

## Glossary

### Basics

- <a id="add-on-cover"></a>**Add-on cover**: Optional extra cover bought on top of the core policy, e.g. accidental damage.
- <a id="excess-deductible"></a>**Excess (deductible)**: The amount the customer pays towards each claim before the insurer pays the rest.
- <a id="exclusion"></a>**Exclusion**: Something the policy specifically does not cover, e.g. wear and tear.
- <a id="policy-wording--schedule"></a>**Policy wording / schedule**: The wording is the full contract (what is and isn't covered); the schedule lists this customer's chosen covers and limits.
- <a id="premium"></a>**Premium**: The price a customer pays for an insurance policy, usually yearly or monthly.
- <a id="sum-insured"></a>**Sum insured**: The maximum the insurer will pay for a policy or item.

### Claims

- <a id="adjuster"></a>**Adjuster**: The claims professional who assesses a claim and decides what the insurer pays.
- <a id="claim"></a>**Claim**: A customer's request for payment after a loss covered by the policy.
- <a id="claims-triage"></a>**Claims triage**: Sorting new claims by complexity and risk: fast-track simple ones, send complex ones to experts.
- <a id="fnol"></a>**FNOL**: First Notice of Loss: the first report of a claim to the insurer.
- <a id="insurance-fraud"></a>**Insurance fraud**: Exaggerating or inventing a claim. Fraud costs insurers billions, raising premiums for everyone.
- <a id="red-flag"></a>**Red flag**: A warning sign of possible fraud, e.g. a claim soon after the policy started or no proof of ownership.
- <a id="siu"></a>**SIU**: Special Investigations Unit: the team that investigates suspected fraud.
- <a id="subrogation"></a>**Subrogation**: Recovering what the insurer paid from a third party who caused the loss, e.g. the at-fault driver's insurer.

### Pricing

- <a id="rating-engine"></a>**Rating engine**: The system that calculates a premium from a base rate and rating factors.
- <a id="rating-factor"></a>**Rating factor**: A characteristic that changes the premium, e.g. driver age, car group or postcode.
- <a id="underwriting"></a>**Underwriting**: Assessing a risk and deciding whether to insure it and on what terms.
- <a id="vehicle-insurance-group"></a>**Vehicle (insurance) group**: A 1-50 scale ranking cars by repair cost, performance and security. Higher groups cost more to insure.
- <a id="voluntary-excess"></a>**Voluntary excess**: An extra excess the customer chooses to pay on claims in exchange for a lower premium.
